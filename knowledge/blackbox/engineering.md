FORMULAS: ΣF_x = 0, ΣF_y = 0, ΣM_O = 0
REBUILD: Draw free-body diagram, resolve forces into x,y components, write equilibrium equations, solve for unknowns

FORMULAS: ΣF_x = 0, ΣF_y = 0, ΣF_z = 0, ΣM_x = 0, ΣM_y = 0, ΣM_z = 0
REBUILD: Isolate body, draw 3D FBD, project forces and moments onto axes, set up six scalar equations, solve

### ENG-07-001
PRINCIPLE: Position, velocity, and acceleration of a particle moving along a straight line are related by derivatives with respect to time.
FORMULAS: v = ds/dt, a = dv/dt = d²s/dt²; v = v₀ + at, s = s₀ + v₀t + ½at², v² = v₀² + 2a(s−s₀)
REBUILD: Measure displacement s(t), differentiate to get v and a, or integrate acceleration to obtain velocity and position

### ENG-07-002
PRINCIPLE: A particle’s motion in space is described by its position vector r(t); velocity and acceleration are its first and second time derivatives.
FORMULAS: v = dr/dt, a = dv/dt = d²r/dt²; in rectangular components: v_x = dx/dt, etc.; normal-tangential: a_t = dv/dt, a_n = v²/ρ
REBUILD: Record trajectory r(t), compute derivatives analytically or numerically to obtain v and a; decompose into tangential and normal components using radius of curvature

### ENG-07-003
PRINCIPLE: The net force acting on a particle equals its mass times its acceleration (∑F = ma).
FORMULAS: ∑F = m a; in components: ∑F_x = m a_x, etc.
REBUILD: Identify all forces, draw free-body diagram, apply ∑F = ma to relate forces to acceleration, solve for unknown motion or forces

### ENG-07-004
PRINCIPLE: The change in kinetic energy of a particle equals the net work done by external forces.
FORMULAS: ΔT = W = ∫ F·dr; T = ½ m v²; W = ∫ F·dr = ∫ F_x dx + F_y dy + F_z dz
REBUILD: Compute initial and final kinetic energies, evaluate work integral of forces along path, equate to find unknown velocity or force

### ENG-07-005
PRINCIPLE: The change in linear momentum of a particle equals the linear impulse of the resultant force.
FORMULAS: ∫ F dt = Δ(m v); linear impulse J = ∫ F dt; momentum p = m v
REBUILD: Determine forces as functions of time, integrate over time interval to get impulse, set equal to momentum change to solve for final velocity

### ENG-07-006
PRINCIPLE: In pure translation, all points of a rigid body have identical velocity and acceleration at any instant.
FORMULAS: v_B = v_A, a_B = a_A for any points A, B; r_B = r_A + r_{B/A} with r_{B/A} constant
REBUILD: Measure motion of one point (e.g., via GPS), infer that every other point moves identically; no rotation needed

### ENG-07-007
PRINCIPLE: A rigid body rotating about a fixed axis has angular position θ(t); all points share same angular velocity ω and angular acceleration α.
FORMULAS: ω = dθ/dt, α = dω/dt; v = ω × r, a = α × r + ω × (ω × r)
REBUILD: Track angular displacement θ(t) (e.g., with encoder), differentiate to get ω and α, compute linear velocities/accelerations of points using radius r

### ENG-07-008
PRINCIPLE: The velocity of any point on a rigid body in plane motion equals the velocity of a base point plus the velocity due to rotation about that point.
FORMULAS: v_B = v_A + ω × r_{B/A}; in scalar form (z‑axis out of plane): v_B = v_A + ω r_{B/A} ⟂
REBUILD: Choose a reference point A with known v_A, measure angular velocity ω, compute cross product to get v_B for any point B

### ENG-08-002

### ENG-08-003
PRINCIPLE: Stress σ is force per area; strain ε is deformation per original length; their relationship defines material stiffness.
FORMULAS: σ = F/A, ε = ΔL/L₀; Hooke’s law: σ = E ε (linear elastic region)
REBUILD: Apply known force, measure deformation, compute σ and ε, plot to obtain Young’s modulus E from slope

### ENG-08-004
PRINCIPLE: Within the elastic limit, deformation is fully recoverable and follows linear proportionality between stress and strain.
FORMULAS: σ = E ε; shear: τ = G γ; bulk: K = −V ΔP/ΔV
REBUILD: Load specimen incrementally, unload, verify zero permanent strain; slope of σ‑ε curve gives elastic moduli

### ENG-08-005
PRINCIPLE: Beyond yield stress, permanent dislocation motion causes irrecoverable deformation.
FORMULAS: Yield criterion (e.g., von Mises): σ_vm = √(½[(σ₁−σ₂)²+(σ₂−σ₃)²+(σ₃−σ₁)²]); flow rule: dε_p = λ ∂f/∂σ
REBUILD: Load past yield, unload, measure permanent set; compare stress at onset of plasticity to theoretical yield

### ENG-08-006
PRINCIPLE: Crack propagation occurs when the stress intensity factor reaches the material’s fracture toughness.
FORMULAS: Mode I: K_I = Y σ √(π a); fracture condition: K_I ≥ K_IC
REBUILD: Introduce a sharp crack, apply tensile load, measure critical load at fracture, compute K_I to obtain K_IC

### ENG-08-007
PRINCIPLE: Under constant stress at high temperature, materials undergo time‑dependent strain.
FORMULAS: Steady‑state creep rate: ε̇ = A σ^n exp(−Q/(RT)); primary, secondary, tertiary stages
REBUILD: Hold constant load at elevated T, record strain vs time, fit to creep law to extract A, n, Q

### ENG-08-008
PRINCIPLE: Repeated cyclic loading below ultimate strength can cause progressive crack initiation and growth.
FORMULAS: S‑N curve: σ_a = σ_f' (2N)^b; Basquin’s law; crack growth: da/dN = C (ΔK)^m (Paris law)
REBUILD: Subject specimen to cyclic stress amplitude, count cycles to failure, plot S‑N; measure crack length vs cycles to get Paris constants

### ENG-08-009
PRINCIPLE: Phase diagrams map equilibrium phases of a system as functions of temperature, pressure, and composition.

REBUILD: Perform thermal analysis (DSC/TGA) at various compositions, plot phase boundaries, construct diagram

### ENG-09-001
PRINCIPLE: Thermodynamics studies energy transfer as heat and work, and the properties of matter in equilibrium.
FORMULAS: none; first law: ΔU = Q − W
REBUILD: Measure heat added Q and work W in a closed system, compute change in internal energy ΔU to verify energy balance

### ENG-09-002
PRINCIPLE: A pure substance has uniform chemical composition; its phases coexist at saturation conditions defined by temperature and pressure.
FORMULAS: Clapeyron equation: dP/dT = L/(T Δv); ideal gas: PV = nRT
REBUILD: Measure boiling point at various pressures, fit to Clapeyron to obtain latent heat L and specific volume change

### ENG-09-003
PRINCIPLE: Work is energy transfer via macroscopic forces; heat is energy transfer due to temperature difference.
FORMULAS: Boundary work: W = ∫ P dV; heat: Q = ∫ C dT (for simple processes)
REBUILD: In a piston‑cylinder, measure pressure‑volume trajectory to compute work; use calorimetry to measure heat transfer

### ENG-09-004
PRINCIPLE: For a closed system, the change in internal energy equals net heat added minus work done by the system.
FORMULAS: ΔU = Q − W; for a simple compressible system: dU = δQ − δW
REBUILD: Conduct a known process (e.g., constant volume heating), measure Q and ΔU (via temperature change and specific heat), verify W≈0

### ENG-09-005
PRINCIPLE: For a control volume, the rate of energy accumulation equals net enthalpy flow in minus out plus heat transfer minus work.
FORMULAS: dE_cv/dt = Σṁ_in h_in − Σṁ_out h_out + Q̇ − Ẇ; steady state: Σṁ_in h_in = Σṁ_out h_out + Q̇ − Ẇ
REBUILD: Measure mass flow rates, inlet/outlet temperatures (to get h), heat input, shaft work; enforce balance to find unknown

### ENG-09-006
PRINCIPLE: The second law states that entropy of an isolated system never decreases; it quantifies irreversibility.
FORMULAS: Clausius inequality: ∮ δQ/T ≤ 0; entropy change: dS = δQ_rev/T; for ideal gas: ΔS = C_v ln(T₂/T₁) + R ln(V₂/V₁)
REBUILD: Operate a reversible cycle (Carnot), measure heat exchanges at temperatures, compute ∮ δQ/T ≈0; for irreversible process, show >0

### ENG-09-007
PRINCIPLE: The Carnot cycle is the most efficient reversible engine operating between two temperatures.
FORMULAS: Efficiency η = 1 − T_C/T_H; COP_R = T_C/(T_H−T_H); COP_HP = T_H/(T_H−T_C)
REBUILD: Execute two isothermal and two adiabatic steps with ideal gas, measure heat absorbed Q_H and rejected Q_C, compute η = 1−Q_C/Q_H

### ENG-09-008
PRINCIPLE: An ideal gas follows PV = nRT, with internal energy depending only on temperature.
FORMULAS: PV = nRT; internal energy U = n C_v T; enthalpy H = n C_p T; specific heats: C_p − C_v = R
REBUILD: Measure pressure, volume, temperature of a known mass of gas, verify PV/nT constant; deduce R

### ENG-09-009
PRINCIPLE: Psychrometrics describes thermodynamic properties of moist air, using dry‑bulb temperature, humidity ratio, and enthalpy.

REBUILD: Measure dry‑bulb and wet‑bulb temperatures, use psychrometric chart or equations to compute humidity ratio, enthalpy, etc.

### ENG-09-010
