## 01 Atomic Structure

CHEM-01-002 | Protons | Positively charged nuclear particles; define the element | Use when identifying elements by atomic number
CHEM-01-003 | Neutrons | Neutral nuclear particles; add mass, affect stability | Use when explaining isotopes and radioactivity
CHEM-01-004 | Electrons | Negatively charged particles in shells around nucleus | Use when explaining bonding and reactions
CHEM-01-005 | Atomic number | Count of protons; fixes an element's identity | Use when reading the periodic table
CHEM-01-006 | Atomic mass | Weighted average mass of an element's isotopes | Use when converting between mass and moles
CHEM-01-007 | Isotopes | Same element, different neutron counts | Use when dating artifacts or tracing reactions
CHEM-01-008 | Electron shells | Energy levels holding 2, 8, 18... electrons | Use when predicting reactivity patterns
CHEM-01-009 | Valence electrons | Outermost-shell electrons that form bonds | Use when predicting how an element reacts
CHEM-01-010 | Ions | Atoms with unequal protons and electrons; charged | Use when explaining salts and electrolytes
CHEM-01-011 | Quantum numbers | Four numbers describing an electron's state | Use when deriving the periodic table's shape
CHEM-01-012 | Orbitals (s, p, d, f) | Regions where electrons are likely found | Use when explaining bond geometry

CHEM-03-004 | Hydrogen bonds | H attracted to N, O, or F on another molecule | Use when explaining water's odd properties and DNA
CHEM-03-005 | Van der Waals forces | Weak attractions between all molecules | Use when explaining boiling points of nonpolar substances

CHEM-03-007 | Bond energy | Energy to break one mole of bonds | Use when estimating reaction heat
CHEM-03-008 | Bond length and strength | Shorter bonds are generally stronger | Use when comparing molecule stability
CHEM-03-009 | Molecular geometry (VSEPR) | Electron pairs repel into shapes | Use when predicting molecule shapes
CHEM-03-010 | Resonance | Delocalized electrons over several structures | Use when explaining benzene or ozone stability

CHEM-04-002 | Molar mass | Grams per mole of a substance | Use in every mass-to-moles conversion
CHEM-04-003 | Balancing equations | Equal atoms each side; mass conserved | Use before any quantitative reaction work
CHEM-04-004 | Limiting reactant | Reactant that runs out first | Use when computing maximum product yield
CHEM-04-005 | Percent yield | Actual over theoretical times 100 | Use when evaluating real reaction efficiency
CHEM-04-006 | Empirical vs molecular formula | Simplest ratio vs true atom counts | Use when identifying unknown compounds
CHEM-04-007 | Concentration (molarity) | Moles of solute per liter of solution | Use when preparing or diluting solutions
CHEM-04-008 | Dilution equation | M1V1 = M2V2 | Use when making weaker solutions from stock
CHEM-04-009 | Titration | Measured neutralization to find concentration | Use when analyzing unknown acid/base strength
CHEM-04-010 | Gas stoichiometry | PV = nRT links gas volume to moles | Use when reactions involve gases

## 05 Thermodynamics
CHEM-05-001 | Enthalpy | Heat content; ΔH negative means exothermic | Use when judging if a reaction gives off heat
CHEM-05-002 | Entropy | Disorder; systems drift toward more of it | Use when predicting reaction spontaneity
CHEM-05-003 | Gibbs free energy | ΔG = ΔH − TΔS; negative means spontaneous | Use as the master test for whether a reaction runs
CHEM-05-004 | Hess's law | Enthalpies add along any path | Use when computing ΔH from known steps
CHEM-05-005 | Calorimetry | Measuring heat flow experimentally | Use when determining reaction energies in the lab
CHEM-05-006 | Bond enthalpies | Sum bonds broken minus bonds formed | Use when estimating ΔH without a calorimeter
CHEM-05-007 | Standard states | Reference conditions for tabulated data | Use when looking up thermodynamic values
CHEM-05-008 | Spontaneous vs fast | Thermodynamics says if, kinetics says how fast | Use when a favored reaction still needs a catalyst

CHEM-06-007 | Reaction mechanisms | Step-by-step molecular pathway | Use when explaining how a reaction really proceeds

CHEM-06-009 | Collision theory | Molecules must collide with energy and orientation | Use when reasoning about concentration and temperature effects
CHEM-06-010 | Equilibrium | Forward and reverse rates equal; concentrations steady | Use when a reaction never fully completes
