## 01 Cell Biology
BIO-01-001 | Cell theory | All life is cells; cells come from cells | Use as the foundation of all biology
BIO-01-002 | Prokaryotic cells | No nucleus; bacteria and archaea | Use when studying microbes
BIO-01-003 | Eukaryotic cells | Nucleus plus organelles | Use for plants, animals, fungi
BIO-01-004 | Cell membrane | Selective barrier of phospholipids | Use when explaining transport and signaling
BIO-01-005 | Nucleus | Holds DNA; control center | Use when discussing heredity and gene expression
BIO-01-006 | Mitochondria | ATP powerhouses via respiration | Use when tracing energy flow in cells
BIO-01-007 | Chloroplasts | Photosynthesis in plants | Use as the base of nearly all food chains
BIO-01-008 | Ribosomes | Build proteins from mRNA | Use when following gene to protein
BIO-01-009 | Endoplasmic reticulum | Protein and lipid factory network | Use when tracing protein production
BIO-01-010 | Osmosis | Water moves across membranes to balance solute | Use when predicting cell swelling or shrinking
BIO-01-011 | Active transport | Pumping against gradients using ATP | Use when cells concentrate substances
BIO-01-012 | Mitosis | One cell divides into two identical | Use when explaining growth and repair
BIO-01-013 | Meiosis | Division making gametes with half the DNA | Use when explaining sexual reproduction and variation

## 02 Genetics and DNA
BIO-02-001 | DNA structure | Double helix of A-T, G-C base pairs | Use as the basis of heredity
BIO-02-002 | Genes | DNA segments encoding proteins | Use when tracing traits to molecules
BIO-02-003 | Chromosomes | Packaged DNA; humans have 46 | Use when discussing inheritance patterns
BIO-02-004 | DNA replication | Semi-conservative copying before division | Use when explaining faithful inheritance
BIO-02-005 | Transcription | DNA copied to mRNA | Use as step one of gene expression
BIO-02-006 | Translation | Ribosomes read mRNA to build proteins | Use as step two of gene expression
BIO-02-007 | Genetic code | 64 codons map to 20 amino acids | Use when reading any gene sequence
BIO-02-008 | Mutations | Changes in DNA sequence | Use when explaining variation, cancer, evolution
BIO-02-009 | Dominant vs recessive | Allele expression patterns | Use when predicting trait inheritance
BIO-02-010 | Punnett squares | Grids predicting offspring genotypes | Use for basic inheritance problems
BIO-02-011 | Gene regulation | Cells switch genes on and off | Use when one genome makes many cell types
BIO-02-012 | CRISPR | Programmable gene editing | Use when modifying genomes precisely
BIO-02-013 | PCR | Amplifying DNA segments exponentially | Use when tiny samples must be analyzed
BIO-02-014 | Sequencing | Reading DNA letter by letter | Use for diagnostics and research

## 03 Evolution
BIO-03-001 | Natural selection | Fittest variants leave more offspring | Use as the engine of adaptation
BIO-03-002 | Common descent | All life shares ancestors | Use when comparing species' traits
BIO-03-003 | Speciation | One lineage splits into two species | Use when explaining biodiversity
BIO-03-004 | Genetic drift | Random allele frequency changes | Use especially in small populations
BIO-03-005 | Fossil record | Preserved remains document deep time | Use as historical evidence of evolution
BIO-03-006 | Homologous structures | Same origin, different function (e.g. limbs) | Use as evidence of shared ancestry
BIO-03-007 | Antibiotic resistance | Selection for resistant microbes | Use when managing drugs and hygiene
BIO-03-008 | Coevolution | Species adapt to each other reciprocally | Use with predators, pollinators, parasites
BIO-03-009 | Extinction events | Mass die-offs reset ecosystems | Use when reading Earth's deep history
BIO-03-010 | Human evolution | Hominin lineage over ~6M years | Use when placing humans in the tree of life

BIO-04-002 | Protein structure | Sequence folds into functional 3D shape | Use when linking gene to function
BIO-04-003 | Enzymes | Protein catalysts; speed reactions enormously | Use when explaining metabolism and digestion
BIO-04-004 | Enzyme inhibition | Molecules that slow or block enzymes | Use as the basis of many drugs
BIO-04-005 | Carbohydrates | Sugars and starches; quick energy | Use when tracing food energy
BIO-04-006 | Lipids | Fats and membranes; dense energy storage | Use when studying membranes and nutrition
BIO-04-007 | Nucleic acids | DNA/RNA polymers storing information | Use at the heart of molecular biology
BIO-04-008 | ATP | Universal energy currency of cells | Use when following any energy-requiring process
BIO-04-009 | Cellular respiration | Glucose plus O2 gives ATP | Use as the core animal energy pathway
BIO-04-010 | Photosynthesis | Light plus CO2 gives sugar plus O2 | Use as the entry of energy into ecosystems
BIO-04-011 | Fermentation | ATP without oxygen | Use when explaining muscle burn and brewing
BIO-04-012 | Vitamins and minerals | Micronutrients enabling enzymes | Use when preventing deficiency disease
